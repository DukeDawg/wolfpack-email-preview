Ask Me to Marry You (The Brides of Evergreen 2)

by Heather Blanton

Two sweet Christian Western romances bring love to Evergreen in unexpected ways: a rancher advertises for a husband to save her home, while a sheriff's long-awaited proposal is threatened by a handsome ghost from the past.

Words from Heather Blanton

When I think back to being a kid and watching some of the best Westerns on TV, I invariably recall two things: Bonanza did comedy well. Gunsmoke did drama the best. Neither of them did romance particularly well, but I'd give the edge to the Cartwright boys. Ask Me to Marry You contains two novellas, both of which have a little humor and more romance than Marshal Dillon ever even thought about. In Male-Order Bride, a marriage of convenience starts with a bride in need of a certain type of groom: desperate. This story was actually inspired by the premise of the old non-Western show Remington Steele. When you read it, you'll see what I mean. And in A Proposal So Magical, hardened marshal Dent Hernandez is in love. Worse, the object of his affection expects him to do something about it. Funny, the things that scare a man. Some would rather shoot their way out of an ambush than ask a gal to marry them. Well, Dent had better saddle up and find some courage, or he's going to lose the girl he loves. I hope you enjoy both these sweet romances! —Heather Blanton

Trials in the Time Between: A Western Double (Ariel's Profit 3)

by JJ Bonham

Across two adventures, Ariel, Profit, and the powerful women beside them confront an ageless realm, dangerous magic, and a shape-shifting enemy who steals their child. The pursuit crosses time and worlds, testing love, power, and destiny.

Words from JJ Bonham

Not all Virgin Bloods are successful in what they do. They don't like to call their first adventures trials, but not calling them doesn't change the danger involved. Those who don't return are stored deep in Urquhart Castle. Well, their spiritless bodies are. The discovery of their suspended bodies sickens Ariel. With her cohorts, she goes to Mag Mell, a mythical place in Ireland where no one ever ages and things seem too good to be true. When things seem too good to be true, they usually are. They are assaulted by young men who see them as new women at Mag Mell. What happens to those lusty young men is not left to your imagination. Finally, the King of Mag Mell, whose Queen is one of the missing Virgin Bloods, decides they're all witches, and the real fun begins. If you've ever dreamed of heaven, that's exactly how this mythical place seems until you look below the surface. Its true nature becomes obvious, but by that time the King has ordered them to be burned at the stake. Join Ariel and her Virgin Blood friends as they navigate treacherous environs and maybe succeed. —JJ Bonham
