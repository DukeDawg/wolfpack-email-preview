Will Parks: Road to Hangtown (Parks Brothers 1)

by Patrick Lindsay

After a Cheyenne attack separates him from his twin on the Oregon Trail, Will Parks pushes toward California's goldfields. Claim jumpers, dock gangs, outlaws, and lawless San Francisco stand between him and the future he hopes to build.

Words from Patrick Lindsay

I live on a cattle ranch south of Fort Worth, Texas, with my wife, Michelle. I've been fascinated by the American West for as long as I can remember. Over the years, I read just about everything Louis L'Amour ever wrote, and that lifelong love of Western fiction eventually sent me traveling across the West, exploring historic forts, mining camps, cattle trails, battlefields, and frontier towns where so much of our nation's history unfolded. The day I retired, I sat down and began writing the kind of Westerns I had always wanted to read. I haven't looked back since. I work hard to make my novels as authentic as possible. Every story is built on extensive historical research, but I also draw from life on our ranch, where cattle, horses, and the land itself are part of my everyday world. My goal is to give readers more than an exciting adventure. I want them to feel the dust of a cattle drive, hear the creak of a saddle, and experience the courage, sacrifice, and determination that shaped the American frontier. When I'm not writing, you'll usually find me working around the ranch or doing some research for my next book. I'm delighted that so many people have enjoyed my work! —Patrick Lindsay

Call of the Wilde: HH Lomax Volume Two

by Preston Lewis

Four rollicking H.H. Lomax adventures send the amiable wanderer from the first herd to Abilene to Alaska, the Pecos, and beyond. Along the way, Wild Bill Hickok, Calamity Jane, Soapy Smith, Judge Roy Bean, and Oscar Wilde turn history into hilarious frontier legend.

Words from Preston Lewis

One of the pleasures of writing the eight comic Westerns in The Memoirs of H.H. Lomax series was that I got to do a lot of historical research, which is always more fun than the writing itself. Some of the research focused on well-known personas and events as the first four books acquainted Lomax with Billy the Kid, Jesse James, the Earps, and George Armstrong Custer. However, the next four books examined some of the more obscure people and events in Western history. All four are now in Wolfpack Publishing's omnibus edition, Call of the Wilde: HH Lomax Volume Two. First Herd to Abilene is the trail-drive novel I always wanted to do. North to Alaska gave me the chance to send Lomax on the Klondike Gold Rush after a date with suffragist Susan B. Anthony. In Outlaw West of the Pecos, Lomax makes enemies of Judge Roy Bean and John Wesley Hardin while helping pull off the most bizarre heavyweight title fight in American history. Finally, in Call of the Wilde, Lomax winds up as bodyguard for poet Oscar Wilde during his speaking tour across the West. When I do research, I'm not only looking for historical facts, but also for odd stories or unknown incidents with comic potential. One such tale involved a questionable escape from a Texas county jail: did the accused break out, or did officials let him go because they couldn't afford a trial? That's how Call of the Wilde begins, with Lomax arrested for a bank robbery he didn't commit and stubbornly refusing every invitation to escape. The more officials try to entice him, the more he resists—until he's in control of the sheriff's office and jail. Ultimately, he does “escape” with help from the sheriff's straight-laced daughter, who has the crookedest teeth in Old West history. And that's all before Lomax meets Oscar Wilde. Check out Volume Two if you're curious who taught Wild Bill Hickok the fast draw, how Lomax's date with Susan B. Anthony saved him from Wells Fargo detectives, how he befriended Judge Roy Bean's pet bear, or what he had to do to guard Oscar Wilde. Lomax may not always show how the West was won, but he does demonstrate how it was fun. —Preston Lewis
