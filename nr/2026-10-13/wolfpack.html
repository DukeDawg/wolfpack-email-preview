Canyon of Silence

by Tony Masero

With his wife and young daughter missing after an Apache attack, Pepper Hinder searches the war-torn Southwest for a way to bring them home. The army’s brutal campaign leads him through ambushes and hidden canyons, until a vital clue forces him to choose between sworn duty, conscience, and the family he refuses to abandon.

Words from Tony Masero

It does not start off too well for the military in Arizona and New Mexico. The eastern seaboard is fully engaged in the Civil War but this far frontier is very much side-lined. I chose to bring this to the front with the story of Pepper Hinder, who begins as a captain in the volunteers. With his beloved wife and baby captured he is torn between the two worlds. Pepper is struggling to find and release his family but at the same time carry out his duties as a soldier engaged in a war with the Indian nations.

The Confederates have been put on the run at Glorieta Pass but they are still a challenge. The Indian question is seen as a tiresome margin and drain on resources by the higher command in Washington who are already facing an extensive war. Under the harsh command of Brigadier Carleton ageing frontiersman Colonel Kit Carson is charged with subduing the Apache and Navajo natives and he does this by following a cruel scorched earth policy. Pepper becomes an officer under him in the assault on the last Navajo redoubt at Chelly Canyon.

It is a hard time in American history and with little chance of respite Pepper must accompany the captured Navajo on their march to the questionable reservation at Bosque Redondo. But even then, there is one source of grief that Pepper must confront before he makes a final decision.

Within the broad sweep of actual history, the story is interwoven with the terrible tests for Pepper Hinder in his struggle against military protocol and affairs of the heart.

Tony Masero
