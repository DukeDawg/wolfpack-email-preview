Playing a Lone Hand

by John D. Nesbitt

Six Western short stories bring frontier justice, ranch life, and small-town conflict into sharp focus. Facing danger on the open range and hard choices closer to home, ordinary men and women discover what courage demands when the odds turn against them.

Words from John D. Nesbitt

Playing a Lone Hand is a collection of short and mid-length fiction. Of these six selections, one won a Western Fictioneers Peacemaker Award and three were finalists.

The opening story, “White City,” told through the eyes of a young narrator, has an investigator on the trail of an abducted girl in a tent city that follows the construction of the railroad. “White City” was a finalist in Best Short Western Fiction in the annual Western Fictioneers Peacemaker Awards in 2025.

“Buckskin Trail” is about a man who looks for a lost horse and tries to solve the murder of a neighboring rancher. It was a finalist in Best Short Western Fiction in the Western Fictioneers Peacemaker Award in 2023.

The third story, “Blood at the Ranch,” is about a young man who learns about a sordid chapter in his father’s past and helps his father deal with it.

“Pearl of Great Price,” the fourth selection, features a sleuth named Willis Thorne, who comes to a small town in Wyoming in search of a girl who has been abducted from an Indian boarding school. Thorne collaborates with a colleague, Miss Greer, and with a man who runs a trading post and has an insect collection.

The fifth story in the collection, “Of Emerald Hue,” also features Willis Thorne. This time he comes to a Wyoming town in search of a rebellious teenage girl who went missing after she came to live with her grandparents. He is on his own in this story, but he meets a woman he thinks he might see later. “Of Emerald Hue” was a finalist in Best Short Western Fiction in the Western Fictioneers Peacemaker Award in 2026.

“Leaving the Lariat Trail” is the final story and the longest one in the collection. In this novella, Charles Landon tells the story of the deadly difficulties he gets into when he decides he wants to get out of the crooked life of rustling cattle. “Leaving the Lariat Trail” won the Western Fictioneers Peacemaker Award in Best Short Western Fiction in 2020.

I would like to express my appreciation to Wolfpack Publishing for showcasing these stories in one place where readers can find them.

John D. Nesbitt
